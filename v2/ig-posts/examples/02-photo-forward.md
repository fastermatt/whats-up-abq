# Example 2 — Photo-Forward Documentary

**Style:** A great photo, no text on the image, the caption does the selling. IG-native, feels like a personal account.

**Best if:** You want @abqunplugged to feel like a *person who lives here* is telling you about shows — not a brand. Think: @thebrooklynorganization, a less-polished @infatuation, local-tipster energy.

---

## Visual Direction

**Canvas:** 1080×1350 (portrait 4:5)

**The image — one of these three, in priority order:**
1. **Best choice:** A real photo from a previous Buckethead show at El Rey (Google Images / reverse search — if there's a great fan shot from 2022 or so, use it with credit). Crowd in silhouette, Buckethead's KFC-bucket silhouette backlit by stage lights. Raw, high-ISO grain. Looks like you were there.
2. **Alternate:** The El Rey marquee at dusk with "BUCKETHEAD TONIGHT" on it. Shot from across the street. Cars, neon, sidewalk.
3. **Fallback:** A clean studio press shot of Buckethead (official). Looks more like a placeholder than a story, but it works.

**Processing:**
- Slight film grain (VSCO A6 or similar)
- Warm shadows (+15), cool highlights (-10) — matches the site's cream/terra palette
- **No text on image.** Not even a date stamp. Let the photo breathe.

**Why it works:** The IG feed is a feed of photos. The best-performing accounts in this space (@dodenverco, @secret_austin) heavily use real, evocative photos without graphic overlays. It reads as "human is here, sharing a thing" not "brand is marketing to me."

---

## Caption (this is where the post lives)

```
Buckethead is playing El Rey tonight.

If you don't know him: he's released more than 400 studio albums. He wears a KFC bucket on his head. He plays the fastest guitar solos you've ever heard. He doesn't talk on stage. He gives out toys to fans in the front row. He is an American treasure.

If you do know him: you already bought the ticket in November.

For everyone else — the show is 8pm, El Rey Theater in Downtown, about $40. It's one of those shows where you'll be telling someone about it a decade from now.

Link in bio.

📍 El Rey Theater · 622 Central Ave SW
🎸 Doors 7pm · Show 8pm
🎟️ Still tickets as of noon

#Buckethead #ElRey #Albuquerque #DowntownABQ #ABQmusic #LiveMusicABQ #BurqueShows
```

**Caption length:** 155 words. First line (truncated in feed preview at ~125 chars) lands the full event info without needing to expand.

**Hook test (first 4 words):** *"Buckethead is playing El"* — direct, specific, and the hook is the event name itself. IG-scroll-safe.

---

## What this style is trading

- ✅ **Authentic.** Feels like a local telling locals. High trust.
- ✅ **Shareable.** People save + DM the post to friends ("check this out") more than poster-style.
- ✅ **Builds a following relationship.** Readers come back to the account, not just individual posts.
- ❌ **Photo bottleneck.** You need great photos. Stock/press photos are fine sometimes, but a diet of press shots makes the account feel like a content mill.
- ❌ **Takes longer to produce.** The caption is the craft. 20–30 min per post.

---

## When this style wins

- Mission posts (the "why we built this" post — photo of Sandias at sunset + long caption)
- Venue spotlights (a warm photo of the inside of El Rey / a barstool at Canteen Brewhouse)
- Event recaps (post-show, next morning — "last night was…")
- Anything community / low-key / free

## When this style loses

- Urgency posts ("tonight only!" — gets buried by a 155-word caption)
- Multi-event roundups (needs carousel)
- Brand-voice establishment (the first 3 mission posts need to LAND the brand — text-on-image helps)

---

## Cadence fit

**2–3 posts/week realistic.** The caption is the work. Skimp on the caption and this style instantly feels cheap. Reserve it for your best events and let the poster style (example 1) carry the mid-week rhythm.
