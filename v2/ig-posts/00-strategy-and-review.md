# Instagram Strategy & Post Review — ABQ Unplugged (@abqunplugged)

*Research completed April 17, 2026. Sources: Hootsuite, Later, Socialinsider, CreatorsJet, Sprout Social, Manychat, and direct observation of comparable accounts.*

## Part 1: Comparable Accounts — What Actually Works

### The Five Accounts Worth Studying

**@secret_nyc — 2M followers**
The closest template for ABQ Unplugged. Their caption formula is almost mechanically consistent: `[LINK IN BIO]` leads every single post title (visible in search results), followed by a short punchy line that reads like a friend texting you. Examples from current posts: *"It's rough out here fr 🥵"*, *"Can you feel our jealousy through the screen?"*, *"Lowkey jealous of Staten Islanders right now."* Notice what's not there: formal event copy, listing details, or anything that sounds like a press release. They run almost entirely Reels now. The hook is emotional or curiosity-based, never informational. Link in bio is the CTA, always. Hashtags: light, usually city-specific only, placed in caption.

**@dothebay — 271K followers, San Francisco Bay Area**
DoTheBay serves 815K+ monthly users. Their Instagram is the social layer on top of a robust event database — very similar to what ABQ Unplugged is building. What's notable: they lean heavily on clean graphic carousels for weekly roundups ("10 things to do this weekend"), with real photos for individual event spotlights. Their captions are tight — usually 2-3 lines max. They use location hashtags reliably (#SFBayArea, #BayAreaEvents) plus category tags (#LiveMusicSF, #FoodiesBayArea). They include CTAs that drive saves: "Save this for the weekend."

**@timeoutnewyork — 773K followers**
Time Out runs a format mix: editorial list carousels ("Best new restaurants this month"), short Reels showing experiences, and single-image event promos. Their captions frequently open with a number or ranking ("The 17 best things to do in NYC this weekend"). This list-opener structure is proven: it promises clear value, rewards saving, and front-loads the payoff in the visible truncated text. They use moderate hashtag counts (8-12), placed in caption.

**@infatuation — 1.6M followers (food-focused city guide)**
Infatuation proves that a confident, opinionated voice outperforms neutral event-listing copy. Their hooks lean on strong adjectives and insider framing: *"The best ramen you haven't tried yet"*, *"This spot changed our minds about [neighborhood]."* They don't try to be comprehensive — they're curators. For ABQ Unplugged, the lesson is: don't just announce the event, have a take on it. Posts 6 and 8 in this batch already do this well.

**@newyorkcity.explore — follower count not confirmed, comparable mid-tier city guide**
Runs a mix of event updates, news-worthy local headlines, food recommendations, and cityscape photos. Appeals to both tourists and locals. Successful because it covers multiple audience segments without losing a coherent voice. Relevant lesson: ABQ Unplugged can serve newcomers, parents, and long-timers simultaneously if the copy always names the specific experience rather than describing an abstract category.

## Part 2: What Works — Actionable Best Practices

### Hook Structure (the only thing that matters in the feed)

Instagram truncates captions at roughly 125 characters in the feed. Everything after that is hidden behind "more." The first line is the only line most users read.

What works:
- **Lead with the "what" at high specificity**: *"Ice Cube. Albuquerque. Saturday night."* beats *"Big concert this weekend!"*
- **Curiosity or surprise**: *"Buckethead is playing El Rey tonight. That's the whole post."* — this structure (statement + meta-acknowledgment) performs well because it feels self-aware and confident
- **Conversational micro-statements**: @secret_nyc's *"Lowkey jealous of Staten Islanders right now"* works because it's human, not brand-speak
- **Avoid**: opening with the account name, a greeting ("Hey Albuquerque!"), a question that's too vague, or any version of "We're excited to share..."

### Caption Length

Data from Socialinsider's 9.1M post study: short captions (under 30 words) produce higher engagement rates; longer captions (400-600 characters) produce more saves and comments. The practical implication for a city events account:

- **Single event spotlights**: keep it tight. Under 100 words. Hook line, 2-3 lines of context, logistics (emoji-formatted), CTA.
- **Weekend roundup carousels**: medium-length is fine. 80-120 words. The carousel itself does the heavy lifting; the caption frames it.
- **Mission/brand posts**: these can go longer — but only if every sentence earns its place.

### Format: Reels for Growth, Carousels for Saves, Single Images for Consistency

From CreatorsJet's 10,000-post study:
- Carousels: 1.38% engagement rate, 12% more interactions than Reels
- Reels: 1.23% engagement rate, but 2.25x the reach of single images for accounts under 50K followers
- Single images: 0.72% engagement rate, simplest to produce

**For a new account with 0 followers, the recommended mix:** 3-4 Reels per week (for discovery/reach), 2-3 carousels per week (for saves/engagement), 1-2 single images (for event-specific promos that don't need the carousel treatment). Start Reel-heavy and shift toward carousels as the audience builds.

Mixed-format carousels (images + video on different slides) hit 2.33% engagement — the highest of any format. Worth experimenting with.

### Text-on-Image

Posts with text overlays get measurably more engagement than image-only posts (85% of Instagram video is watched without sound — text provides the essential context). For ABQ Unplugged's brand:
- Event title + date in Epilogue 900 on cream or terra background = clear, ownable look
- Keep text in the middle 60% of vertical space (grid preview crops top and bottom)
- Limit to 2-3 lines of text on image — let the caption do the storytelling
- Consistent graphic templates for recurring content types (weekend roundups, tonight picks, venue spotlights) builds recognizability fast

### Hashtag Strategy

Later's analysis of 18M+ posts shows approximately 20 relevant hashtags produces the strongest reach, while Instagram officially recommends 3-5. The practical sweet spot for a local account: **8-12 hashtags**, mixing three tiers:

- **Location-specific** (always): #Albuquerque, #ABQ, #NewMexico, #505, #Burque
- **Category-niche** (match to post): #ABQEvents, #NMMusic, #AlbuquerqueEats, #ABQArts, #ThingsToDoABQ
- **Broad discovery** (1-2 only): #CityGuide, #LiveMusic, #WeekendEvents

**Placement**: put hashtags in the first comment to keep captions clean, or group them after a line break at caption bottom. Either works; first comment is cleaner visually.

### CTA Patterns That Convert

Ranked by what drives algorithm signals for a city events account:

1. **"Save this for the weekend"** — highest save rate, directly asks for the most valuable signal
2. **"Tag who you'd bring"** — drives comments and reach through tagging
3. **"Link in bio for tickets"** — drives traffic (use for time-sensitive events)
4. **"Swipe for the full lineup"** — carousel engagement signal
5. **"Drop your pick in the comments"** — generates comment volume for list posts

Avoid: generic "like and share," passive "check it out," or double CTAs in a single post. One ask per post.

### Post Frequency

For a new account: 3-5 posts per week produces 2x the follower growth rate versus 1-2 posts per week. The ceiling is 5-6 before quality starts to suffer. Recommended starting cadence: **5 posts/week** — 2 Reels, 2 carousels, 1 single image/graphic.

Best posting times for engagement: Thursday 4-5 PM, Wednesday 12 PM, Friday 4 PM. Post event-specific content 48-72 hours before the event, not morning-of.

### Post Type Mix (Rough Target)

| Type | Frequency | Purpose |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Weekend roundup carousel (5-7 events) | 1x/week | Save-bait, high value |
| Individual event spotlight | 2x/week | Ticket traffic, event partners |
| "Tonight" single-image or Reel | 1x/week | Urgency, local feel |
| Brand/mission post | 1x/2 weeks | Audience education |
| Venue/neighborhood spotlight | 1x/2 weeks | Community depth |
| UGC re-post or community content | As available | Trust + authenticity |

## Part 4: Post-by-Post Notes and Rewrites

### Post 1 — "What this is" (Score: 5.6) — REWRITE

**What's wrong:** "Hey Albuquerque" is the weakest possible hook. It's a greeting, not a hook. The first visible line is swallowed by pleasantries before the actual value prop lands. The "1,464 upcoming events" stat is buried at the bottom — that's your hook. Also, 90% of people on Instagram don't care about the back-end problem (Ticketmaster vs. Eventbrite) — they care about the output: *what do I do tonight*.

**Rewrite:**

> 1,464 events in Albuquerque. One place to find them all.
>
> Concerts, comedy shows, food festivals, sports, library storytimes, things your kid wants to do, things you meant to do last month.
>
> No more bouncing between five ticketing sites. It's all here.
>
> Link in bio → abqunplugged.com

*Visual note: Put "1,464 events" large on the graphic. Update the number as it grows. The specificity is the credibility.*

### Post 2 — "Why we built this" (Score: 6.2) — REWRITE

**What's wrong:** The Harvard Study and Surgeon General citations are correct and interesting, but they're a slow open for Instagram. You're asking the reader to sit through two data points before getting to the point. The real hook is the last paragraph — *"Pick one. Bring a person. That's the whole plan."* — that line should open this post, not close it.

**Rewrite:**

> Pick one. Bring a person. That's the whole plan. 🌆
>
> In 2023 the US Surgeon General declared loneliness a public health emergency. The 85-year Harvard study on adult development found that relationships at 50 predict your health at 80 better than your cholesterol.
>
> The fix is mostly just: show up. Go to the thing. Talk to someone.
>
> We built ABQ Unplugged to make that one step easier. Every event in the city. One place.
>
> abqunplugged.com — link in bio.

*Visual note: "Pick one. Bring a person." large on the graphic, cream background, terra type.*

### Post 3 — "Who this is for" (Score: 5.8) — REWRITE

**What's wrong:** The list is good in concept but too long (6 bullet points) for a caption that has to hook in 125 characters. In the feed, the viewer sees: *"This isn't for scrolling. It's for: • The Friday you realize you have nothing planned • The new dad..."* and then it cuts. The list doesn't build — it just stacks. Two or three of these are doing the heavy lifting; cut the rest. The 4-tile collage visual is the right call for this post.

**Rewrite:**

> This isn't for scrolling. It's for doing something.
>
> • The Friday you realize you have nothing planned
> • The new mom who needs something that starts at 10 and ends before naps
> • The person who just moved here and doesn't know where the city gathers yet
> • Anyone whose friends all cancelled at 6:30
>
> One site. Every event in Albuquerque. Link in bio.

*Cut the teenager bullet (it reads as filler). Cut the last meta-bullet (redundant — the whole post already says this). Four bullets is the ceiling for this format.*

### Post 10 — San Holo @ Revel (Score: 5.8) — REWRITE

**What's wrong:** The hook buries what makes this show interesting. "Electronic music at Revel tonight" is genre-and-venue, which tells a non-fan nothing. More critically, "If you're in the 18-28 range" is the wrong move — you're immediately excluding people and signaling to everyone else that this isn't for them. Let the event sell itself; if the crowd skews young, they'll know. The bitbird / guitar-led future-bass description is good but it's in the wrong place — that's the hook material.

**Rewrite:**

> San Holo plays guitar in a DJ set. Tonight. At Revel. 🎸
>
> His sound is layered and live — future-bass with actual strings, not just a laptop set. If you've never seen someone make electronic music feel human, this is the show.
>
> 📍 Revel Entertainment Center
> 📅 Tonight (Apr 17)
>
> Link in bio for tickets.

## Part 5: 3 Additional Post Concepts to Add

**1. "Best things happening this weekend" carousel (weekly, every Thursday PM)**
Format: 5-7 slides. Slide 1: "This weekend in Albuquerque 🗓️" on branded cream/terra graphic. Slides 2-6: one event per slide, photo + event name + date/venue in Epilogue text. Final slide: "All events at abqunplugged.com — link in bio." CTA in caption: "Save this for the weekend." This is your highest-save content type and the most shareable format a city guide can run. DoTheBay does a version of this; it's consistently their top performer.

**2. "Venue spotlight" single image or short Reel (biweekly)**
Format: one Albuquerque venue per post — El Rey, Marble Brewery patio, Isotopes Park, the Bosque at sunset, KiMo Theatre. Real photo, minimal text overlay. Caption focuses on the venue's character, not a specific event: *"El Rey Theater has been hosting shows since 1941. Small enough to be in the room. Big enough to book names. Tonight: Buckethead."* This builds local pride, educates newcomers, and performs well as shareable content for people tagging friends.

**3. "Last-minute pick" story-style post (Friday afternoon, every week)**
Format: single image or very short Reel. Cream background. One event. Ultra-short copy: *"Nothing planned tonight? → [Event name, time, venue]. Details at the link in bio."* This serves the specific audience segment of Post 3 ("the Friday you realize you have nothing planned") and trains followers to check the account before making Friday night decisions. Over time, this becomes a habit loop.
